# Source Verification Dossier: Complete Aging-in-Place Checklist

Status: Verification plan and claim map. External sources still need live verification before publication.

This dossier identifies the claims, source types, and publication blockers for the flagship article and master checklist.

## Publication rule

Do not publish the cornerstone article or public PDF until the required claims are checked against current sources.

## Required source targets

Use current versions of these source categories before publication:

1. National Institute on Aging home safety / aging in place guidance
2. CDC fall prevention resources
3. Administration for Community Living / Eldercare Locator caregiver resources
4. AARP family caregiving resources
5. Occupational therapy or certified aging-in-place professional guidance
6. Product/manufacturer documentation for any specific safety product claims
7. CPSC or recall-related resources for product safety concerns where relevant

## Claim-to-source map

### Claim group A: Falls and home hazards

Claims to verify:

- Falls are a major safety concern for older adults.
- Clear walking paths, lighting, stair safety, bathroom safety, and trip-hazard reduction are common home-safety priorities.
- Recent falls or near-falls should trigger a more careful safety review.

Best sources:

- CDC fall prevention resources
- National Institute on Aging home safety resources
- Occupational therapy home assessment resources

Draft locations:

- Direct answer
- What to fix first
- Room-by-room checklist
- Professional-help triggers

### Claim group B: Bathroom safety

Claims to verify:

- Bathrooms are high-priority areas for safety review because of wet surfaces, transfers, and hard surfaces.
- Grab bars, shower chairs, transfer benches, and toilet-height considerations may be useful in some situations.
- Towel bars and non-weight-rated fixtures should not be relied on for body-weight support.
- Suction-only products should be discussed carefully and not treated as equivalent to professionally installed support.

Best sources:

- Occupational therapy resources
- Manufacturer installation instructions
- NIA or other aging-in-place safety resources
- CPSC/recall sources if specific product categories are discussed

Draft locations:

- Bathroom checklist
- Common mistakes
- Product guidance notes

### Claim group C: Bedroom and nighttime safety

Claims to verify:

- Clear paths, reachable lighting, reachable phone/alert device, and consistent placement of mobility aids can help reduce nighttime confusion and hazards.
- Bed rails require careful product-specific and person-specific consideration.

Best sources:

- NIA home safety guidance
- Occupational therapy resources
- CPSC/medical-device safety resources for bed rail concerns

Draft locations:

- Bedroom section
- Professional-help triggers

### Claim group D: Kitchen and fire/cooking concerns

Claims to verify:

- Reducing climbing/reaching, improving item placement, and addressing unattended cooking concerns are practical safety topics.
- Repeated cooking issues may require professional/family review.

Best sources:

- NIA home safety guidance
- Fire safety resources such as NFPA or local fire safety agencies
- Dementia/alzheimer's organization sources if memory-related cooking risks are discussed

Draft locations:

- Kitchen section

### Claim group E: Medications and health information organization

Claims to verify:

- Caregivers should keep an up-to-date medication list.
- Medication questions should be directed to a pharmacist or qualified clinician.
- Medication routines should not be changed without professional guidance.

Best sources:

- NIH/NIA medication safety resources
- Medicare or healthcare organization patient-safety resources
- Pharmacist or clinical sources

Draft locations:

- Medication and appointment organization
- Caregiver binder section

### Claim group F: Emergency readiness

Claims to verify:

- Emergency contact lists, visible house numbers, access instructions, charged phones, and key documents can help families and responders respond faster.

Best sources:

- Ready.gov / FEMA emergency preparedness resources
- Local emergency-services guidance
- ACL caregiver resources

Draft locations:

- Emergency-readiness checklist

### Claim group G: Professional-help triggers

Claims to verify:

- Occupational therapists, primary care clinicians, pharmacists, geriatric care managers, certified aging-in-place specialists, contractors, and elder law attorneys can each be appropriate depending on the situation.
- The article should not imply one professional can solve every case.

Best sources:

- AOTA or occupational therapy resources
- ACL / Eldercare Locator
- AARP caregiving resources
- State bar or elder-law resources for legal-topic framing

Draft locations:

- When to call a professional
- FAQ

## Claims to avoid until verified

Do not publish exact statistics unless freshly sourced:

- Percent of older adults who fall
- Number of fall-related hospitalizations or deaths
- Average costs of falls or home modifications
- Product-specific effectiveness rates
- Medical-alert response-time claims
- Medicare/insurance coverage claims

## Editorial changes needed before publication

- Add live citations or source notes under the sources section.
- Add a stronger product-safety note before recommending any support device.
- Keep the flagship article mostly non-commercial.
- Move specific product buying advice into separate product decision guides.
- Add a visible disclaimer block.
- Add reviewer line if expert review is obtained.

## Expert review target

Preferred reviewers:

- Occupational therapist with older-adult or home-safety experience
- Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist
- Geriatric care manager
- Home modification professional

Suggested review questions:

1. Are any checklist items misleading, unsafe, or too broad?
2. Are the professional-help triggers appropriate?
3. Are any product categories oversimplified?
4. Should any items be moved from general checklist to professional assessment only?
5. Are there missing high-priority home-safety areas?
