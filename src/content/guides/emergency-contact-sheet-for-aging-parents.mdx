## Direct answer

An emergency contact sheet should include the older adult's name, address, primary and backup contacts, nearby helper, primary care office, pharmacy, preferred hospital, allergies, key medical notes, home access instructions, and date last updated.

The goal is to help trusted people act quickly when something changes.

## Privacy note

Only share sensitive contact, access, medical, or legal information with trusted people who need it. Keep the sheet somewhere accessible enough to be useful but not careless with private details.

## What to include first

Start with:

- Full name
- Address
- Phone number
- Date the sheet was last updated
- Primary emergency contact
- Backup family contact
- Nearby local helper
- Primary care office
- Pharmacy
- Preferred hospital
- Allergies or urgent medical notes
- Home access instructions for trusted helpers

## Add local support contacts

Depending on the situation, also include:

- Home care agency
- Paid caregiver or care coordinator
- Area Agency on Aging
- Transportation contact
- Meal delivery contact
- Faith/community support contact
- Pet-care contact
- Landlord, building manager, or maintenance contact if relevant

## Home access notes

Write only what trusted helpers need. Examples:

- Who has a spare key
- Who should be called first after a missed check-in
- Whether there is a lockbox or access plan
- Where mobility aids, hearing aids, glasses, chargers, and emergency supplies are kept
- Pet instructions

## Pair this with emergency planning

Ready.gov recommends older adults and caregivers assess needs, make a plan, build a support network, include medicines and medical supplies, copy important cards, and make sure at least one support-network member knows where emergency supplies are located.
