## Direct answer

A caregiver medication list should track medication name, dose, schedule, prescriber, pharmacy, refill notes, allergies, adverse reactions, and questions for a clinician or pharmacist. It should help people communicate clearly, not change medications on their own.

## Safety note

This guide is for organization only. It is not medical advice. Do not start, stop, split, combine, skip, or change medications without guidance from a qualified clinician or pharmacist.

## What to include on the list

For each medication, write down:

- Medication name
- Dose
- Schedule
- Prescribing clinician
- Pharmacy
- Reason it was prescribed, if known
- Refill date or refill question
- Side effects or concerns to ask about
- Whether it is prescription, over-the-counter, vitamin, supplement, cream, inhaler, drop, or injection

## Do not forget non-prescription items

Medication conversations can get confusing when over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, supplements, sleep aids, pain relievers, allergy medicines, and herbal products are left off the list.

Add a section for:

- Over-the-counter medicines
- Vitamins
- Supplements
- Herbal products
- Sleep aids
- Pain relievers
- Cold or allergy products
- Topical creams or patches

## Allergy and reaction section

Keep a separate area for:

- Known drug allergies
- Past adverse reactions
- Medication combinations that caused problems
- Side effects that need a follow-up call
- Questions for the pharmacist or clinician

MedlinePlus notes that medicines can have risks, side effects, drug interactions, allergies, and interactions with foods, supplements, or medical conditions. That is why the list should be used to support professional conversations, not replace them.

## When to ask a pharmacist or clinician

Ask for professional guidance if:

- A dose was missed or doubled.
- There are new symptoms after a medication change.
- The person is more dizzy, confused, sleepy, weak, or unsteady.
- Multiple clinicians prescribe medications.
- Over-the-counter medicines or supplements are being added.
- A refill is unclear.
- Pills look different after a pharmacy refill.
- The person has trouble following the schedule.

## How often to update the list

Update the list after:

- Any new prescription
- Any stopped medication
- Hospital or rehab stays
- Emergency room visits
- Pharmacy changes
- New allergies or reactions
- New supplements or over-the-counter medicines
- Major changes in memory, balance, appetite, sleep, or alertness
