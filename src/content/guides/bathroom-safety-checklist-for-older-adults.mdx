## Direct answer

Bathroom safety starts with the areas where wet surfaces, transfers, low lighting, and unstable support can create problems. Review the path to the bathroom, tub or shower surface, toilet transfers, lighting, reach zones, and whether any support equipment should be evaluated by a qualified professional.

## Safety note

This guide is general education. It is not individualized safety, medical, occupational therapy, construction, or installation advice. Bathroom modifications and support products should match the person, the home, and the surface where they are used or installed.

## First things to check

- Is the path to the bathroom clear and well lit?
- Is the bathroom floor frequently wet or cluttered?
- Is the tub or shower surface slippery?
- Does the person hold towel bars, counters, shower doors, or nearby furniture for support?
- Are toiletries reachable without bending, twisting, or standing on tiptoe?
- Is the toilet height manageable?
- Is there a reliable way to call for help from the bathroom?

## Shower and tub checklist

- Review whether the person can step in and out safely.
- Check whether the surface feels slippery when wet.
- Keep soap, shampoo, towels, and other items within easy reach.
- Avoid clutter around the tub or shower edge.
- Discuss shower chairs or transfer benches with a qualified professional if bathing feels unstable.
- Review product weight limits, instructions, and fit before using any bathroom safety product.

## Grab bar caution

Grab bars can be helpful when properly selected and installed, but they are not interchangeable with towel bars or decorative fixtures. Do not assume an existing bar, suction product, or temporary support can safely hold body weight.

If grab bars may be needed, consider professional guidance on placement, wall structure, installation method, and the person's actual movement pattern.

## Toilet area checklist

- Check whether sitting down and standing up feels difficult.
- Check whether the person reaches for unstable objects for support.
- Review whether surrounding space is clear enough for mobility aids.
- Consider whether toilet-height changes or nearby support should be discussed with a professional.

## Nighttime bathroom trips

- Add reachable lighting near the bed.
- Keep the route to the bathroom clear.
- Reduce loose rugs, cords, and clutter along the path.
- Consider motion lighting if the person gets up at night.
- Keep mobility aids in a consistent location.

## When to ask for professional help

Consider professional help if:

- There has been a recent fall or near-fall in or near the bathroom.
- The person struggles to step into the tub or shower.
- The person needs to pull on fixtures for support.
- A shower chair, transfer bench, grab bar, raised toilet seat, or major modification is being considered.
- There are memory, judgment, dizziness, weakness, or medication concerns affecting bathroom routines.
