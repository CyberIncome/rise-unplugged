## Direct answer

The easiest way to coordinate care between siblings is to stop relying on vague promises. Write down the recurring tasks, assign one owner and one backup for each, set a check-in rhythm, and keep shared notes somewhere everyone can access.

## Start with tasks, not emotions

Family caregiving can bring up old patterns, guilt, resentment, and different ideas about what should happen. Start with the visible work first.

List what needs to happen:

- Appointments
- Transportation
- Medication or pharmacy pickups
- Groceries and meals
- Bills and paperwork
- Home maintenance
- Weekly check-ins
- Emergency calls
- Backup or respite care

## Assign one owner and one backup

For each task, write:

- Primary person
- Backup person
- Frequency
- What counts as done
- How updates will be shared

A task without an owner usually becomes a crisis later.

## Use a shared system

Use one shared place for:

- Emergency contacts
- Medication list
- Appointment notes
- Care calendar
- Home access notes
- Professional contacts
- Open questions

This can be a binder, shared document, spreadsheet, or care app. The format matters less than whether everyone actually uses it.

## Create a missed-check-in plan

Write down what happens if:

- The older adult misses a call.
- A sibling cannot complete their task.
- A caregiver gets sick.
- A ride falls through.
- A medication pickup is missed.
- There is a fall, hospital visit, or urgent change.

## Keep meetings short

A weekly or biweekly check-in can be simple:

1. What changed?
2. What is urgent?
3. What needs scheduling?
4. Who owns the next task?
5. What needs professional help?

## What to avoid

- Assuming one person will keep carrying everything.
- Using group texts as the only care system.
- Waiting until people are angry to assign tasks.
- Hiding important information from siblings who need it.
- Ignoring the older adult's preferences.
