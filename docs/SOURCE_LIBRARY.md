# Source Library

This is the working source library for Aging at Home Lab. It identifies source categories and priority references to consult before publishing sensitive content.

Important: articles should use current, verified sources at publication time. This file is a planning library, not a substitute for fresh source checks.

## Source quality tiers

### Tier 1: Primary and highly authoritative

Use for health, safety, demographic, government-program, and emergency-readiness claims.

- CDC
- NIH / National Institute on Aging
- Administration for Community Living
- Medicare.gov
- Consumer Product Safety Commission
- U.S. Census Bureau
- Bureau of Labor Statistics
- National Fire Protection Association
- FEMA / Ready.gov
- State and local aging agencies where relevant

### Tier 2: Professional and nonprofit sources

Use for practical aging, caregiving, occupational therapy, home modification, and family-caregiver context.

- AARP
- National Alliance for Caregiving
- American Occupational Therapy Association
- Area Agencies on Aging
- Rebuilding Together / home modification resources
- National Council on Aging
- Alzheimer’s Association for dementia-specific resources
- Family Caregiver Alliance

### Tier 3: Product and manufacturer documentation

Use for product specs, limitations, warranty details, installation requirements, and compatibility.

- Product manuals
- Manufacturer safety instructions
- Installation guides
- Warranty pages
- Recall notices
- Retail listings only as secondary checks, not primary evidence

### Tier 4: User experience and market signal sources

Use for pain mining, feature gaps, user complaints, and article angle discovery. Do not treat as medical or safety evidence.

- Amazon reviews
- Reddit caregiver communities
- YouTube comments
- Forum discussions
- Product review comments
- BBB complaints where relevant

## Claims that need strong sources

- Fall statistics
- Hospitalization or injury statistics
- Medication safety
- Dementia wandering or cognitive decline
- Driving safety
- Legal documents and authority
- Medicare/Medicaid/payment claims
- Product safety standards
- Home modification installation guidance
- Emergency preparedness guidance

## Topics that likely need expert review

- Medication management systems
- Fall-risk assessment
- Dementia-related safety
- Bed rails and restraint-adjacent products
- Transfer benches and mobility aids
- Stair lifts and ramps
- Hospital discharge planning
- Power of attorney and legal documents
- Care agency selection
- Any topic involving individualized safety decisions

## Default source workflow

1. Identify the claim type.
2. Use Tier 1 sources for facts that affect safety, health, law, money, or public statistics.
3. Use Tier 2 sources for caregiver context and professional frameworks.
4. Use product manuals for product-specific claims.
5. Use reviews/forums only to understand user problems and decision criteria.
6. Add source notes to the article brief.
7. Verify freshness before publication.

## Source notes by pillar

### Home safety

Likely source categories:

- CDC fall prevention resources
- National Institute on Aging home safety resources
- Occupational therapy home assessment resources
- CPSC product safety or recall information
- Manufacturer installation instructions for safety products

### Caregiver organization

Likely source categories:

- AARP caregiving reports and guides
- National Alliance for Caregiving research
- Family Caregiver Alliance resources
- ACL caregiver support resources
- Medicare planning resources where relevant

### Senior technology

Likely source categories:

- Product manuals
- Manufacturer pages
- FTC/consumer protection resources for subscriptions or claims
- Privacy policies for monitoring products
- Medical alert system terms and limitations

### Home modifications

Likely source categories:

- Occupational therapy sources
- Aging-in-place home modification resources
- ADA-related public guidance where relevant, with care not to over-apply commercial/residential contexts
- Manufacturer installation guides
- Contractor/professional association resources

### Legal and financial planning

Likely source categories:

- Government sources
- State-specific resources if applicable
- Elder law attorney review
- Medicare.gov for Medicare-specific claims
- Avoid giving legal/financial advice directly

## Citation discipline

Every published article should include:

- Source links or notes for material claims
- Last-reviewed date
- Clear distinction between general education and individualized advice
- Professional-help triggers for sensitive decisions
