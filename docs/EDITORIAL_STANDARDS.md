# Editorial Standards

Aging at Home Lab covers topics that may affect health, safety, money, legal planning, independence, and family decision-making. Trust is the product.

## Content promise

Every published guide should help the reader make a safer, clearer, more organized decision.

A guide should not exist just because a keyword exists.

## Required checks before publishing

### Practical usefulness

- Does the article answer a real problem?
- Does it give the reader a next action?
- Does it include a checklist, template, comparison table, decision tree, diagram, or other original asset when appropriate?
- Would a caregiver bookmark it?

### Sourcing

- Are important factual claims supported by credible sources?
- Are health/safety claims sourced from government, medical, nonprofit, academic, professional, or manufacturer documentation where appropriate?
- Are commercial claims separated from editorial claims?

### Safety boundaries

- Does the article avoid personalized medical, legal, financial, or safety advice?
- Does it explain when to call a qualified professional?
- Does it avoid implying that a product can eliminate risk?
- Does it avoid fear-based or guilt-based persuasion?

### Affiliate and monetization transparency

- Are affiliate relationships disclosed clearly near relevant recommendations?
- Are product recommendations based on transparent criteria?
- Are sponsored relationships labeled?
- Does the article still help readers who do not buy anything?

### Freshness

- Does the article include a last-reviewed or last-updated date?
- Does it identify product specs, prices, policies, or programs that may change?
- Is there a refresh plan for policy/product-heavy articles?

## Risk levels

### Low risk

Examples:

- Printable organization templates
- General room-by-room clutter reduction
- Non-medical caregiver planning worksheets
- Basic home organization

Review requirement: standard editorial review.

### Medium risk

Examples:

- Fall-risk reduction checklists
- Medical alert systems
- Home monitoring devices
- Grab bars, ramps, shower chairs
- Care agency comparison questions

Review requirement: stronger sourcing and safety notes. Expert review preferred for major guides.

### High risk

Examples:

- Medication management
- Dementia wandering
- legal/POA planning
- financial benefits
- discharge planning
- individualized safety decisions

Review requirement: expert review or very careful framing as questions to ask qualified professionals.

## Default disclaimer language

Use plain, calm language. Example:

> This guide is for general education and organization. It is not medical, legal, financial, or individualized safety advice. For decisions that affect health, medications, legal authority, home modifications, or personal safety, talk with a qualified professional.

## Voice

- Calm
- Practical
- Respectful
- Not alarmist
- Not patronizing
- Clear enough for an overwhelmed family member
- Specific enough to be actionable

## Banned patterns

- Fear-based product pushing
- Fake urgency
- Unsupported medical claims
- Legal conclusions without attorney review
- Financial advice without qualification
- Overpromising safety outcomes
- Thin AI summaries
- Articles that mostly rewrite other sites

## Preferred article features

- Direct answer near the top
- Who this guide is for
- What to do first
- Checklist or table
- When to call a professional
- Common mistakes
- FAQ
- Sources
- Last-reviewed date
