# Aging at Home Lab Brand Strategy

## Working brand

**Aging at Home Lab**

## Tagline options

1. Practical checklists and home-safety systems for families helping aging parents stay safer at home.
2. Clear, practical guides for aging in place without chaos.
3. Home-safety checklists, caregiver tools, and decision guides for aging at home.
4. The practical field guide for families helping older adults stay safer at home.

## Recommended positioning

Aging at Home Lab helps family caregivers and adult children make aging-in-place decisions with less panic and more structure.

The site should feel like a calm, practical workbench: checklists, diagrams, decision trees, questions to ask professionals, product comparisons, and printable tools.

## Core audience

Primary reader:

- Adult child or family caregiver helping an aging parent, spouse, relative, or neighbor stay safer at home.
- Often overwhelmed, short on time, and unsure what to fix first.
- May be coordinating with siblings, doctors, home-care providers, contractors, or paid caregivers.
- Needs practical structure more than inspirational advice.

Secondary reader:

- Older adult planning ahead.
- Spouse caregiver.
- Home modification professional looking for clear consumer-facing education.
- Occupational therapist, care manager, or aging-services professional who may share practical resources.

## Emotional state of the reader

The reader may be worried, guilty, frustrated, confused, or exhausted. They may be searching after a fall, hospital stay, near-miss, medication mistake, or argument about independence.

The content should reduce panic and create next steps.

## Editorial voice

- Calm
- Practical
- Respectful
- Specific
- Non-alarmist
- Non-patronizing
- Evidence-aware
- Plain English

## Brand promise

Aging at Home Lab turns overwhelming aging-in-place decisions into practical checklists, safer home systems, and clearer family conversations.

## What makes us different

Most aging-in-place content is either too generic, too clinical, too sales-driven, or too scattered.

Aging at Home Lab should combine:

- Room-by-room practical checklists
- Printable caregiver tools
- Product decision criteria
- Clear safety boundaries
- Professional-help triggers
- Source-backed explanations
- Calm family communication guidance
- Transparent affiliate/product-review standards

## Content moat

The site should not depend on generic advice. Every core guide should include at least one original asset:

- Printable checklist
- Decision tree
- Product comparison worksheet
- Room-by-room audit
- Family conversation script
- Caregiver binder page
- Professional questions list
- Diagram or visual map

## Brand design direction

Visual tone:

- Warm
- Clean
- Trustworthy
- Not medical-cold
- Not infantilizing
- Not luxury-senior-living glossy

Suggested palette direction:

- Warm off-white background
- Deep forest or slate green for trust and calm
- Soft beige/tan for warmth
- Muted blue for information blocks
- Gentle amber for warnings or priority actions

Suggested typography direction:

- Highly readable sans-serif for body text
- Warm serif or humanist sans for headings
- Large type, generous line height, strong contrast

## Trust pages needed

- About Aging at Home Lab
- Editorial Policy
- Medical/Legal/Financial Disclaimer
- Affiliate Disclosure
- Product Review Methodology
- Corrections Policy
- Expert Review Policy

## Monetization boundaries

Allowed if transparent and useful:

- Affiliate links for home safety products
- Affiliate links for monitoring and medication-management tools
- Printable templates and paid caregiver binders
- Newsletter sponsorships from relevant, vetted companies
- Lead generation only when partners are clearly vetted and disclosed

Avoid:

- Fear-based medical alert selling
- Products with exaggerated safety claims
- Hidden sponsorships
- Payday-style financial offers
- Legal/medical conclusions without qualified review
- Anything that exploits caregiver panic

## Brand north star

If an overwhelmed family member lands on one page at midnight after something scary happened, the page should help them breathe, understand what matters, and take the next sane step.
