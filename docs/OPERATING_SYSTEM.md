# Aging at Home Lab Operating System

Aging at Home Lab is a practical media and product project focused on helping families make safer, clearer, better-organized aging-in-place decisions.

## Core mission

Help adult children, family caregivers, and older adults answer practical questions like:

- What should we fix first to make the home safer?
- What products are actually useful versus fear-based upsells?
- How do we organize medications, appointments, emergency contacts, and care responsibilities?
- When should we call a professional?
- How do we compare options without getting overwhelmed?

## Operating model

The project is run as a research-backed publishing lab.

Each major content asset should go through this path:

1. **Topic selection** — chosen from urgent caregiver/home-safety problems, not generic SEO volume alone.
2. **Intent mapping** — define exactly what the reader is trying to accomplish.
3. **Risk rating** — determine whether the content touches health, safety, legal, financial, or medical decisions.
4. **Source gathering** — collect credible sources before drafting.
5. **Original asset planning** — checklist, decision tree, diagram, template, comparison table, or printable PDF.
6. **Drafting** — produce a practical, human-usable guide.
7. **Editorial review** — check clarity, claims, safety boundaries, sourcing, and affiliate disclosures.
8. **Expert review when required** — occupational therapy, elder law, home modification, geriatric care, medical, or financial professional depending on topic.
9. **Publishing** — ship article with metadata, internal links, structured sections, source notes, and last-reviewed date.
10. **Refresh loop** — revisit articles based on product changes, policy changes, search data, and reader feedback.

## Content categories

- Home safety
- Caregiver organization
- Senior technology
- Home modifications
- Product decision guides
- Family coordination
- Emergency readiness
- Printable tools and templates

## Trust rules

- Never pretend to provide medical, legal, financial, or individualized safety advice.
- Cite strong sources for factual claims.
- Be clear when professional help is needed.
- Avoid fear-based selling.
- Mark content with last-reviewed dates.
- Clearly disclose affiliate relationships.
- Use expert review for sensitive topics.

## First strategic objective

Build the most useful beginner-to-intermediate aging-in-place resource library around practical home safety and caregiver organization.

The first pillar should be the **Complete Aging-in-Place Checklist**, supported by room-by-room checklists, caregiver binder templates, and product decision guides.
