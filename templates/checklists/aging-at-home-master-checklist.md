# Aging at Home Master Checklist

Status: Draft template. Requires source verification and expert review before being used as a public downloadable resource.

Use this checklist to organize a first pass through an older adult's home. It is not a professional home assessment and does not replace medical, occupational therapy, legal, financial, or contractor advice.

## How to use this checklist

- Start with obvious hazards and urgent needs.
- Do not try to fix the entire home in one day.
- Write down questions for professionals.
- Involve the older adult as much as possible.
- Review after a fall, hospital stay, medication change, mobility change, or new diagnosis.

## Priority 1: Fix obvious fall and emergency risks

- [ ] Clear main walking paths.
- [ ] Remove loose clutter from floors.
- [ ] Check for loose rugs or curled rug edges.
- [ ] Improve lighting in hallways, stairs, bedroom, bathroom, and entryways.
- [ ] Make sure frequently used items are reachable without climbing.
- [ ] Confirm working smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms where appropriate.
- [ ] Confirm the older adult can call for help from bedroom, bathroom, and main living area.
- [ ] Place emergency contact information somewhere easy to find.
- [ ] Check whether exterior doors, locks, and keys are manageable.
- [ ] Make sure house numbers are visible for emergency responders.

## Bathroom

- [ ] Check for slippery tub or shower surfaces.
- [ ] Check whether a shower chair or transfer bench may be needed.
- [ ] Check whether properly installed grab bars may be needed.
- [ ] Avoid relying on towel bars or suction-only devices for body weight support.
- [ ] Add night lighting between bedroom and bathroom if needed.
- [ ] Keep toiletries within easy reach.
- [ ] Check whether the toilet height is manageable.
- [ ] Check whether bath mats create trip hazards.
- [ ] Keep the floor dry and uncluttered.
- [ ] Identify whether a professional home-safety assessment is needed.

## Bedroom

- [ ] Make sure there is a clear path from bed to bathroom.
- [ ] Add reachable lighting near the bed.
- [ ] Keep phone, emergency button, or call device reachable from bed.
- [ ] Check bed height: too high or too low can make transfers harder.
- [ ] Remove loose cords or clutter around the bed.
- [ ] Keep glasses, hearing aids, walker/cane, and medications in consistent places.
- [ ] Consider whether nighttime motion lighting would help.
- [ ] Review bed rails carefully with a professional if considering them.

## Kitchen

- [ ] Move frequently used items to waist-to-shoulder height.
- [ ] Reduce the need for step stools.
- [ ] Check appliance controls for readability and ease of use.
- [ ] Keep floors clear and dry.
- [ ] Check whether heavy pans or dishes are stored safely.
- [ ] Confirm food storage and expiration-date routines.
- [ ] Review fire safety and unattended cooking concerns if relevant.
- [ ] Make sure emergency contacts are visible or accessible.

## Stairs, hallways, and entryways

- [ ] Check stair lighting.
- [ ] Check handrails on both sides where possible.
- [ ] Check for loose or uneven steps.
- [ ] Keep stairs free of clutter.
- [ ] Add contrast markings if step edges are hard to see.
- [ ] Check entryway thresholds for trip hazards.
- [ ] Make sure porch, driveway, and walkway lighting is adequate.
- [ ] Check whether railings, ramps, or threshold modifications may be needed.

## Living areas

- [ ] Create wide, clear walking paths.
- [ ] Remove or secure loose cords.
- [ ] Check furniture stability.
- [ ] Make sure chairs are easy to get in and out of.
- [ ] Keep remote, phone, glasses, and important items within easy reach.
- [ ] Reduce low tables or objects that are easy to trip over.
- [ ] Check whether pets create trip risks.

## Medication and health information organization

- [ ] Create a current medication list.
- [ ] Include medication name, dose, schedule, prescribing doctor, and reason if known.
- [ ] Include allergies and adverse reactions.
- [ ] Include pharmacy name and phone number.
- [ ] Keep a list of doctors and specialists.
- [ ] Track upcoming appointments.
- [ ] Ask a pharmacist or qualified clinician about medication questions.
- [ ] Do not change medication routines without professional guidance.

## Emergency readiness

- [ ] Create an emergency contact sheet.
- [ ] Include family contacts, neighbors, doctors, pharmacy, preferred hospital, and home access instructions.
- [ ] Confirm how emergency responders can enter if needed.
- [ ] Create a basic hospital go-bag or document folder.
- [ ] Know where insurance cards and ID are kept.
- [ ] Keep a flashlight available.
- [ ] Confirm phone charging habits.
- [ ] Discuss what should happen after a fall, missed call, or emergency.

## Caregiver coordination

- [ ] Identify who handles appointments.
- [ ] Identify who handles medications or pharmacy pickups.
- [ ] Identify who handles groceries and meals.
- [ ] Identify who checks the home environment.
- [ ] Identify who manages bills, paperwork, or professional appointments if applicable.
- [ ] Create a shared calendar or check-in routine.
- [ ] Write down what the older adult wants help with and what they do not want help with.
- [ ] Schedule regular review conversations instead of waiting for crisis moments.

## Professional-help triggers

Consider professional guidance if any of these are true:

- [ ] There has been a recent fall or near-fall.
- [ ] Walking, transfers, or stairs have become harder.
- [ ] There are medication mistakes or confusion.
- [ ] There are memory, wandering, or judgment concerns.
- [ ] The bathroom feels unsafe.
- [ ] A ramp, stair lift, grab bar, or major modification is being considered.
- [ ] The older adult is returning home after a hospital or rehab stay.
- [ ] Family members disagree about what level of help is needed.
- [ ] The older adult lives alone and has trouble calling for help.
